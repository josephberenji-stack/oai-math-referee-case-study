# M1 and M2:  where the headline statements' meaning comes from (28 Oct 2026)

Method:  `scripts/StmtOrigins.lean.tmpl`, one read-only Lean pass per family on the compiled solution module.  It walks
the headline statement through the types and values of every definition it uses (stopping at `opaque` values) and
lists the definitions declared in OpenAI's `OAI` namespace.  Each is then matched against the challenge file's own
declarations (`census.stated_names`).  Raw lists:  `results/origins-*.txt`.

| challenge | file imports | OAI definitions in the statement's meaning | of those, declared in the challenge file | solution-side only |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| PeriodicGroup | Mathlib | 14 | 7, plus 7 generated by the challenge's own `deriving Group` | 0 |
| OddKaplansky | Mathlib | 4 | 4 | 0 |
| CartierChartCompactnessSupport | `OAI` | 18 | 0 | **18** |
| CharacterVarietiesAllSeamsSupport | `OAI` | 599 | 0 | **599** |

**M1 holds.**  Both challenges that import OpenAI's library have solution-side definitions inside their statement's
meaning:  18 for Cartier (`closedChartFieldRetraction`, `chartCompletionMap`, ...), 599 for CharacterVarieties.  For
these two, what Comparator fixes as "the statement" is largely defined by the library under test.

**M2 holds on the two Mathlib-only challenges built.**  Every OAI definition in their statements is the challenge
file's own:  OddKaplansky's 4 directly;  PeriodicGroup's 7 directly and 7 more that Lean generates from the challenge's
`deriving Group` on `Steinberg` (the instance `instGroupSteinberg` and its auxiliaries), which a plain name match
misses.  This is evidence for Comparator's design where the challenge imports only Mathlib, and is reported as such.

So the slide, where it exists, is at the 2 of 418 challenges whose statement imports the solution's library.  The other
measured gap (M3) is the challenge-local definitions themselves:  9 and 4 here, read by a person against the paper.
